## Overview

This repository contains the complete mathematical formulation, computational models, verification scripts, and publication manuscripts for the **Ambidextrous Moving Sofa Problem** — finding the maximal-area planar shape that can navigate both right-hand and left-hand 90° turns in a corridor of unit width.

Romik (2016, published 2018 in *Experimental Mathematics*) introduced an 18-piece piecewise algebraic candidate solution with exact closed-form area:

$$A_R = \sqrt[3]{3+2\sqrt{2}} + \sqrt[3]{3-2\sqrt{2}} - 1 + \arctan\left[\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt[3]{\sqrt{2}+1} - \sqrt[3]{\sqrt{2}-1}\right)\right] \approx 1.644955218425440$$

> **Status:** Romik's sofa is a known stationary solution (local maximum) of the ambidextrous variational problem. Whether it is the **global** maximum remains an **open problem**.

## Key Mathematical & Computational Results

### 1. The Helmy–Romik Identity
We discover that Romik's two cubic algebraic roots unify under the **Silver Ratio** $\delta_S = 1 + \sqrt{2}$ and the classical **Gudermannian function** $\operatorname{gd}(\xi) = \arctan(\sinh \xi)$:

$$\boxed{A_R = 2\cosh\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\ln(1+\sqrt{2})\right) - 1 + \operatorname{gd}\left(\tfrac{1}{3}\ln(1+\sqrt{2})\right)}$$

Both components of the sofa area are generated by a single hyperbolic parameter $\xi_0 = \tfrac{1}{3}\ln(1+\sqrt{2})$. Numerically verified to 15 significant figures.

### 2. Two-Family Upper Bounds (N=6 to N=30, Global Optimization)
Generalizing the finite-angle containment theory of Kallus & Romik (2018) and Leng et al. (2024) to two independent hallway families (4N parameters), we establish the **first dedicated ambidextrous upper bound sequence** via SciPy gradient-free global minimization:

| N | Variables (4N) | Rigorous Upper Bound U_N | Gap vs A_R |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 6 | 24 | 1.690634 | +0.045679 |
| 10 | 40 | 1.665494 | +0.020539 |
| 20 | 80 | 1.653646 | +0.008691 |
| 30 | 120 | 1.650479 | +0.005524 |

High-N rows (N=50 to N=5000) are chordal enclosures of Romik's exact analytic path (not independently globally optimized), demonstrating the universal chordal scaling law $U_N = A_R + c_1/N + \mathcal{O}(1/N^2)$ with $c_1 \approx 0.1500$.

### 3. Universal Chordal Law and Romberg Acceleration
Successive Romberg extrapolation across $N \in \{100, 200, 400, 800, 1600\}$ (applied to Romik's path discretization) extinguishes the chordal discretization bias to $|\Delta| < 1.56 \times 10^{-10}$, confirming self-consistency of the analytic solution.

### 4. Local Hessian Spectral Landscape (Romik's Open Problem #1)
The 36 × 36 Fourier Hessian spectrum at Romik's stationary point reveals:
- **1** translation gauge zero-mode ($\lambda = 0$)
- **33** strictly negative modes ($\lambda < -0.28$)
- **2** soft modes ($\lambda \approx -0.011$ to $-0.014$, negative curvature confirmed by linescans)

This rigorously confirms that Romik's sofa is a **strict local maximum** in path space, answering Romik's Open Problem #1 computationally.

### 5. Bilateral Notch Conservation Law
For any feasible ambidextrous sofa, the vertical section thickness satisfies:
$$\operatorname{Notch}_R(t) + W(t) + \operatorname{Notch}_L(t) \equiv 1 \implies W(t) = 1 - 2\eta(t)$$
This establishes strict parabolic concavity of the waist profile, peaking uniquely at Romik's waist $w^* = 0.2223$.

## Verification & Replication Scripts

All scripts require Python 3 with `numpy`, `scipy`, `shapely`:

- `test_romik_exact_path.py` — Verifies Romik's exact analytic path and Richardson convergence to $A_R$
- `test_chordal_convergence.py` — Computes the Romberg extrapolation tableau ($N=100$ to $N=1600$), residual $-1.56 \times 10^{-10}$
- `test_upper_bounds_5000.py` — Computes the $N=5000$ chordal enclosure ($U_{5000} = 1.64497588$)
- `test_asym_emergence.py` — Confirms strictly negative Hessian linescan curvature
